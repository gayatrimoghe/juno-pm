RocketShip already has P0–P3 written down, with explicit inclusions and exclusions. P0 is trust, reliability and compliance. Platform & Infra can't take new read or write volume on the reporting table until the Q4 sharding ships, and phase 1 of the export migration is already committed for Q3. The oversold features (Sales snapshot). A rep told Acme we have real-time Slack notifications. We don't. Another told them TikTok integration is coming. Nobody has discussed it. Gavin named an AI-powered dashboard in a press interview before anything was scoped. This is a signal class my Juno can't handle, because it isn't feedback. Sarah's interview is a CSV crash, a nav bar complaint and a dark mode request in one transcript. My constraint says don't merge complaints. The inverse needs saying too: split one record across themes, and count her once in each theme she actually belongs to. Pearson shows the other half of this, appearing as a support ticket, an at-risk renewal, and the reason for a Q4 roadmap item. Still one customer.. Juno does this inside limits a human set on purpose, and defends every call it makes.

## 01 Context · Data Requirements

**Required sources, and exclusions**


Signal sources, each scoped to the digest window:

- Slack: `#cs-escalations`, `#support-firehose`, `#backend-monitoring` — messages posted inside the window only
- Jira: the onboarding project (ONB), tickets created or commented in the window, body plus comments, not the ticket's whole history
- Notion: the customer feedback log, rows dated in the window
- The support queue: ticket subject, body, account name
- CRM: the call-notes field, flagged accounts only, using the Sales Ops Pipeline Snapshot as the flag list
- Research artifacts I drop in the shared feedback folder, like user interview transcripts

Three reference documents, read as reference and never counted as a signal, pulled live at run time:

- RocketShip Strategy One-Pager, the Strategic Priority Framework section, for P0–P3
- RocketShip Roadmap Q3/Q4, for what's already committed
- Engineering Capacity Note Q3 (Priya Nair, VP Eng), for what's blocked

**Excluded**

- **ARR, deal size, renewal value.** Juno reads Pearson Co's blocker, not the $50k attached to it. If it can't see the money it can't quietly rank by it, and I don't have to trust it not to.
- **Contract, legal and regulator items.** The vendor liability-cap clause routes to me untouched. Those need a signature, not a theme.
- **DMs and private channels.** Only the three named channels. A complaint that exists only in a DM isn't evidence I can put in front of anyone in planning.
- **Anything outside the window.** A theme's count is this week's count. History lives in the carry-forward list, not in a re-scan, so a count can't quietly inflate by reaching further back.
- **Customer identity past the account name.** "The Pearson Co admin," not Mike Ross. This gets read out in a room.
- **Exec statements and press as feedback.** Gavin's email and the press interview aren't customer signal. They land in "Promised, not built," where the claim is the subject rather than the evidence.

**How fresh must it be**

I want a weekly snapshot, cut Sunday at 23:59 and delivered 8am Monday. If a customer says something Tuesday afternoon, I'm fine waiting until the next Monday to see it. That's the trade I'm making on purpose — I want one message showing up at one time, and I know myself well enough to know I'd stop reading something that updated all week.

The three reference docs are my exception. I want those pulled fresh every run, never cached. If the roadmap is stale, Juno tells me a theme is already committed when it isn't, and that's the one mistake that would make me stop trusting everything else in the message. I'm accepting the side effect: a theme can go from committed to slipped between two Mondays with no new customer signal behind it.

**When a source is unreachable**

If a signal source goes down — Jira, one of the Slack channels, the CRM — I want it to retry twice over about five minutes, then run without it and tell me in the window line. *Sep 22–29, 11 sources, Jira unreachable.* I want every count that week marked as a floor rather than a total, and I want it to say that out loud. I can still use a digest with a hole in it as long as I know where the hole is. What I can't use is one that looks complete and isn't.

If a reference doc is down, I want it to stop. No cached framework, no roadmap from memory. One line: *Couldn't produce this morning's digest, the priority framework was unreachable.* Letting it assign priority from a framework it can't open is the same problem as letting it make up a citation — it would look fine to me, and I'd have nothing to check it against.

## 02 Tools · System Capabilities

**The verb list**

- `read_signals(window, source)` — READ. Messages, tickets and rows from one named source inside the window. Returns records with id, account, timestamp, body.
- `read_framework()` — READ. The Strategic Priority Framework section of the strategy one-pager, pulled live.
- `read_roadmap()` — READ. Q3 committed, Q3 expected-but-uncommitted, Q4 planned, with owner and status.
- `read_capacity_note()` — READ. Which pods are blocked and on what.
- `read_memory()` — READ. Theme names, already-counted signal ids, the carry-forward Unclear and Left out lists, open "promised, not built" items.
- `group_signals(signal_ids, theme_name)` — DRAFT. Assigns signals to a theme. The customer count comes out of the distinct accounts in this list. I never let it pass a count in as a number, because then the number and the evidence can disagree.
- `match_priority(theme_id)` — DRAFT. Returns P0–P3 plus the framework clause it matched. No clause, no priority — it returns `unclear` instead.
- `match_roadmap(theme_id)` — DRAFT. Returns the initiative name and status, or `none`.
- `draft_digest(window, themes, promised, split, left_out, unclear)` — DRAFT. Assembles the message and runs the format checks. Fails loudly rather than trimming to fit.
- `deliver_to_pm(digest)` — WRITE. My DM, and only my DM. The channel is a constant in the code, not an argument, so there's nothing to pass that would send it somewhere else.
- `write_memory(theme_names, counted_ids, carry_forward, promised_open)` — WRITE. Its own state, nothing else.

**Deliberate omissions**

No post_to_channel(). Not a narrower version either, no allowlist of safe channels. The second this thing can land somewhere other than my DM it stops being a draft and becomes a publication, and a count that's wrong turns into something a team acts on before I've read it. The DM is my review step. I also made the destination a constant in the code rather than a parameter, so there's no argument anyone can pass later to get around it.

No notify_stakeholder() for "promised, not built." This is the one I actually wanted. A rep told Acme we have real-time Slack alerts that nobody built, and the obvious move is to ping Jessica. But that's a correction about something a human said to a customer, arriving from an AI, and if Juno has it wrong she now owns an awkward conversation she didn't need to have. The whole point of that section is to get my attention, so it comes to me and stops there.

No suggest_fix() or rank_by_impact(). Not because they're dangerous. Because they're my job. Everything else on the list is a lookup against a document somebody else wrote, which is exactly why I can check it. A ranking has no document behind it, so there's nothing to check it against, and I think it would quietly become the thing people read instead of the counts.

No write_jira() or update_notion(). Reversible on paper, since you can delete a comment. Not reversible in practice. A comment from Juno on ONB-412 gets read, replied to and acted on long before anyone goes back to check whether the count behind it held up.

## 03 Loop · AI Costs & Latency

**Turn ceiling and escalation**

12 tool-calling turns per run. One pass over the sources, one grouping pass, one verification pass, with room to spare. At 12 it sends what it has, labelled turn limit reached, counts are incomplete. It doesn't quietly trim the digest to fit.

2 retries per unreachable signal source, over about five minutes, then it drops that source and names it in the window line.

1 retry on a failed format check. Second failure, it stops and sends one line.

What hands it back to me

Regardless of where it is in the 12:

A reference doc is unreachable after 2 retries. No cached framework, no roadmap from memory. One line and stop.
Fewer than 3 sources in the window, or every signal from a single account. Nothing produced.
A theme matches two framework clauses at different priorities. If the same theme reads P0 under one clause and P2 under another, the framework didn't settle it and I don't want Juno settling it either. Goes to Unclear with both clauses named.
The roadmap and the capacity note disagree. An initiative marked committed this quarter, owned by a pod the capacity note says is blocked. It stops tagging that theme either way and flags the conflict. That's the Acme Slack-alerts problem in general form, and honestly it's the thing I most want this to catch.

**Latency and cost target**

**Latency**

It runs at 8am on a schedule with nobody watching, so speed isn't really my constraint. Under 90 seconds end to end is plenty. The only deadline that matters is that the message is sitting there when I open Slack.

**Cost**

This is the part I did arithmetic on, because turns multiply rather than add. Twelve turns across a week of signals — call it 300 to 600 records, plus three reference docs re-read every run — lands around $0.40 to $0.90. I'd set the ceiling at $1.50 a run.

At one run a week that's about $6 a month. Nobody needs to approve $6, which is sort of the point. It replaces 45 minutes of my Monday, and I don't have to defend my estimate to anyone to make that math work.

Where it stops being free: twelve PMs running this daily is roughly $540 a month, and the three reference docs get pulled 60 times a week instead of once. That's the point where someone will suggest caching the roadmap to save money. I want it written down now that we shouldn't. A stale committed tag is the failure I care most about, and $540 isn't enough to justify bringing it back.

## 04 Memory · Data Requirements

**What persists, at what scope**

Everything is scoped to the digest, not to a person and not to a customer. One store, belonging to the onboarding digest:

Theme names. A canonical list, so the SSO theme is the same theme next week. Without it I can't tell 4-went-to-6 from a new theme that happens to sound similar.
Which signals it already counted, and into which theme. An escalation thread that stays hot all month shouldn't be fresh evidence every Monday.
The Unclear and Left out lists, with the date each item first appeared. That's the only way I'd notice something has been sitting in Unclear since August.
Open "promised, not built" items — what was said, who said it, who they said it to.
The roadmap snapshot it last compared against, so it can tell me when something moved from committed to slipped.
Last week's digest, so I can diff.

**Expiry and write rules**

Nothing is scoped to a customer. I don't want Juno building a picture of Pearson Co that outlives the signals in front of it. If Pearson complains three weeks running, that's three themes with Pearson in the count, not a profile somewhere saying Pearson is difficult. Same for Sarah: her interview feeds themes and then it's done.

If another PM runs their own digest, their store is separate and nothing crosses. A theme name I set for onboarding has no business showing up in someone else's area, and a count built from my sources shouldn't be visible in theirs.

And nothing Juno worked out on its own persists. A guessed cause, a hunch about what's really going on, a fix it thought of. If it isn't in a source, it dies when the run ends.

## 05 Permissions · AI Risks & Mitigations

**The permission tier**

| Side-effect class | Tier |
|---|---|
| read | auto |
| draft | auto |
| write | auto |
| send | blocked |

**Justification**

**Write on auto — the one I'd actually have to defend.**

Two writes exist. Delivering to my own DM, and Juno updating its own memory.

*DM delivery.* Blast radius is one person: me. Undo is me not reading it. Confirming it would mean a prompt asking whether I want to receive the thing I scheduled, which isn't a safety check, it's friction I'd learn to click through. What's actually holding this line isn't the tier anyway — the channel is a constant in the code, not an argument, so there's no value anyone can pass to make it go somewhere else.

*Memory write.* Blast radius is next Monday's digest. Undo is one edit, by me, and I'd catch it because every stored thing shows up in the output: a bad theme name is visible in the table, a mis-filed signal id shows up as a count that looks wrong when I open the row. It's the rare case where the system that writes the state and the system that displays it to the person who can fix it are the same thing.

I'd move write to confirm the day `post_to_channel()` exists. It doesn't, which is the reason auto is defensible here and wouldn't be in most products.

**Send on blocked rather than confirm.**

Blast radius is a customer, a rep, or an engineer, and the recoverability is zero in the way that matters — you can delete a Slack message but you can't un-read it. The tempting case is pinging Jessica about the Slack alerts she oversold. If Juno is right, I can tell her myself in ten seconds. If Juno is wrong, she's had an accusatory message from a bot about a conversation she had with a customer. Bad upside, bad downside.

Confirm would technically cover that, but confirm means the capability is built and sitting there, and I'm one tired Monday click from using it. Blocked means there's no button.

**Read on auto, briefly, because the CRM one isn't obvious.**

Most reads are harmless — wasted tokens, about 40 cents. The CRM call-notes field is the exception, since that's rep commentary written without expecting an audience. I scoped it to accounts Sales Ops already flagged rather than the whole pipeline, and I kept ARR out at the source rather than at the permission level, because a dial someone can turn up later isn't a guarantee.

## 06 Verification · AI Testing & Measurement

**The check before output ships**

Six gates, all mechanical, all run against records pulled this session. Nothing passes on a judgment call.

Quote fidelity. Every quote has to be a literal substring of a record Juno read this run, exact match on the trimmed string. A paraphrase fails. A tidied-up version of what someone said fails. A quote that's verbatim but comes from a record outside the window fails.
Counts are derived, not asserted. The customer number is computed from the distinct accounts in the signal list attached to that theme. There's no code path where a count gets written separately from its evidence, so the two can't drift apart.
Sources resolve. Every link and Jira key maps back to a record id Juno actually opened this run.
Priority cites a clause. Every P0–P3 carries the framework clause it matched, pulled verbatim from the framework read this session. No clause, no priority — the theme goes to Unclear instead.
Committed tags match the roadmap pulled this session, on both the initiative name and its status.
Format holds. Five rows maximum, priority inside P0–P3, no signal dated outside the stated window, all the sections present.

The bar is all six, every run. There's no partial credit and no weighted score — any one of them failing means the run failed, it retries once, and then it tells me.

What I like about this set is that none of it requires Juno to assess its own work. Every gate is a string comparison or a lookup against a document someone else wrote, which is why I can trust the gates even when I don't yet trust the judgment behind them.

**Failure behaviour**

One retry. If it fails a second time, I get one line and nothing else:

Couldn't produce this morning's digest. Quote fidelity failed on "SSO setup fails or needs hand-holding."

Which gate, which theme. No partial table, no "here's what I had anyway." If I open Slack and see four themes, I need to know that's four themes and not six with two quietly dropped.

The rule I care most about: it never writes a better-sounding citation to get past a gate. If no framework clause covers a theme, the answer is Unclear, not the closest-sounding clause. If the quote it wanted isn't verbatim in the record, it picks a different quote or the theme goes to Unclear. Reaching for something that passes is the one behaviour that would make every other check here worthless, because a gate you can satisfy by trying harder isn't a gate.

Degraded isn't the same as failed, and I want those handled differently. A missing signal source isn't a gate failure — it runs, names the source in the window line, and marks every count a floor instead of a total. A missing reference doc is a stop, because priority and committed tags have nothing to check against.

And nothing gets dropped quietly, ever. A theme that fails a gate shows up in Unclear with the reason attached. A run that fails entirely still sends me a message. What I need on a Monday morning is for silence to mean nothing happened — not that something happened and went wrong.
